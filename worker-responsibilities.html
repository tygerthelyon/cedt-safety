OHSA Section 28

The duties of a worker are explicitly defined in the OHSA. The worker is required to:

  • work in compliance with all prescribed workplace health and safety directives;
  • use protective equipment and devices as prescribed;
  • report to the supervisor any equipment defects;
  • report to the supervisor any workplace hazard.

According to the OHSA, Section 1(1), a Worker means "a person who performs work or supplies services for monetary compensation." This includes:

  • Laboratory Supervisors
  • Teaching Assistants (TAs)
  • Post-Doctoral Faculty
  • Research Engineers and Technical Staff
  • Research Associates/Fellows
  • Graduate Students (not technically employees but nonetheless Workers)
  • Summer Student Researchers

Workers have certain responsibilities, outlined in Section 28 of the OHSA. In practice, these responsibilities amount to taking prescribed training and completing documentation, working in accordance with written Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs), and reporting hazards to the appropriate supervisor so the situation can be corrected. Site-Specific Training

Additional Site-Specific Training may be mandatory (e.g., Biosafety Training, Basic Electricity, Gas Cylinder, Laser Safety, Machine Guarding) to access equipment or activities in a faculty research lab. For any such task, an SOP should already exist and be made available to you. Please contact your supervisor for more information.

Mandatory Documentation

  • Job Hazard Analysis
    Stipulated by UHS and to be completed and thoroughly reviewed with your supervisor.
  • Hazard Orientation Safety Report form
