Three scenarios…

You're working with HF. A bottle is knocked over and spills a small puddle of acid on:

  1. the floor of the lab space;
  2. your protective glove;
  3. your unprotected skin, just above your glove and below your lab coat.

Which of these would be considered an "incident"?

Answer: each of the three scenarios is, in fact, considered an incident and would require follow up.

What should I do next?

  1. Case 1. Secure the spill area so no one else is at risk.
    Case 2. Wash the acid off the glove, then remove and dispose of the gloves.
    Case 3. Wash the acid off skin and immediately apply calcium gluconate gel. Immediately seek professional medical attention. The risk of complication is serious and real.
  2. Inform the lab supervisor, and if necessary, assist them with clean up.
  3. Make sure that the supervisor files an Incident Report.

An "incident" refers to any accident or injury, or to any situation that could potentially lead to an accident or injury.

In the event of an incident, all employees are required to inform their supervisor so that an Incident Report can be filed.

Reports are to be filled out within 24 hours of an incident, either by a worker directly involved in the incident, a worker witness to the incident, or a supervisor of the workplace in which the incident occurred.

Note that certain serious injuries fall under the definition of a "critical injury". In the event of a critical injury, a supervisor must immediately notify UHS and ensure that the scene of the incident remains undisturbed until formally inspected.

Why Fill Out an Incident Report?

Incident reports serve a number of purposes. First and foremost, they provide a record of the incident so that, in the event of claims or lost work-time, appropriate benefits and compensation can be provided. Also, incident reports may help to identify specific hazards or trends that could otherwise escape detection, providing motivation to remedy or improve work conditions.

Incident reports should be filed for any physical injury, no matter how apparently insignificant.
